penTell / temporally deniable honey encryption

The secret that waits to become itself

Crack this blob early and you get a convincing decoy. The real secret does not exist yet. It emerges in final form only when a public clock fires, and only for the holder of a separate key. Same bytes, two doors.

A chrysalis: what goes in as a caterpillar comes out, at the right time, as a butterfly.

Now wait for the clock

The true secret is timelocked to a future round of a public randomness beacon. Its key does not exist until the round fires, so nobody opens it early, not even the sealer. And even after it fires, opening needs a separate holder shard the thief never had.

The same trick beats a real guard

contextSeal is a deny-by-default guard that inspects everything an agent sends and blocks secrets. Wrap the secret in a Chrysalis shell first and the guard sees only gibberish, because the real secret is not there yet. The fix is not smarter reading, it is refusing to forward what you cannot read.

Verified against the real contextSeal authorize(). Break, then fix, then attack the fix. That is what penTell is for.

What it is not

  • Strong for tight secrets like keys and tokens. Not for whole free-form documents, where a good fake is hard.
  • No help if the attacker owns your machine at the moment you open the real thing.
  • If the beacon goes dark and never publishes the round, the true secret is unrecoverable, for everyone.
  • The timelock uses pairing crypto that is not post-quantum. It guarantees time, not quantum-proof secrecy.
